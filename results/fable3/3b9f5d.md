Observations:
All numbers below are from the LayerNorm-folded weights (W_q/W_k/W_v rows multiplied by ln1_w of the layer), QK bilinear forms M_h = W_q_h^T W_k_h / 8, and OV_h = W_o_h W_v_h.

1. Layer-0 attention is almost entirely one sharp head plus four near-uniform "averaging" heads.
   - L0H2 is the only strong QK circuit in layer 0: top singular value of M = 1.415 with 17 singular values above 0.3*sv1 and 22 above 0.1*sv1 (|W_q|=11.5, |W_k|=13.3 folded). Its Q and K read the same 22-dim input subspace (row-space overlap 0.73; top-24 U/V overlap 0.78), the form is nearly normal (||MM^T-M^TM||/||M||^2 = 0.19) and its eigenvalues come as conjugate pairs with |lambda| matching the singular values (1.03, 0.96, 0.94, 0.85, 0.82, 0.77, 0.61, 0.51, ...) at spread phases (52, 104, 157, 69, 32, 56 deg...). That is the signature of a bilinear form built from 2-D rotation planes, i.e. a relative-position (Fourier-type) attention pattern on the positional embedding, with a peak away from offset 0 (the self term, trace = +1.5, is far from its maximum ~14). Its output goes to a private residual subspace (overlap with every other L0 output 0.01-0.07).
   - L0H0, H1, H4, H5 have essentially no QK circuit: top singular values 0.051, 0.025, 0.089, 0.076 (20-30x smaller than H2), effectively rank 1-2. Their attention is therefore (close to) uniform over all positions 0..i. Their OV circuits are copies of each other (cosine similarity between OV matrices 0.72-0.90; V row-space overlap ~0.4, W_o output overlap 0.43-0.47 vs 0.04 random) and are essentially rank-1/2: the stacked V of the four heads has singular values 3.55, 0.77, 0.54, ... and their summed OV has singular values 22.5, 13.9, 5.9, 1.5. The averaged input directions lie 98% and 85% inside the 22-dim subspace that L0H2 uses for its positional Q/K (the top one loads on H2's dominant rotation plane, the 52-degree pair), and MLP0 barely reads them (0.7x rms). So these four heads compute the running mean over the context of two low-frequency positional-embedding components, i.e. a smooth scalar function of absolute position, and dump it with large gain into a handful of residual dims (dim 222 gets 20.95 of a norm-22.3 output; then dims 78, 360, 72). Layer 1's LN bias has its largest entries exactly there (ln1_b.1[222] = -1.47, [360] = +1.15, [78] = +0.87).
   - L0H3 is weak (sv1 = 0.359, next 0.119) and symmetric (real eigenvalues 0.262, 0.078 at phase 0); its key side is 91% the same positional direction (|v1.d0| = 0.87), so it is a soft positional prior, not a content head.

2. Layers 1-3 key on that averaged position signal, not on token identity of a previous character.
   - K-composition scores (||W_k W_OV|| relative to a random rotation baseline): L1 heads vs the four averaging heads: 8-15x; L2 heads: 4-8x; L3H2/H3/H4: 3-5x; vs L0H2 (the sharp head) only 0.9-1.6x in every layer. Directly, all six L1 keys read the main averaged direction at 10.8-11.9x their rms column norm (second direction 7.9-8.5x), L2 keys at 4.9-7.2x, L3H2/3/4 at 3.9-5.3x, while the corresponding queries read it at only 0.8-2.0x. In layer 1 the whole top-of-QK lies on this: removing the three averaged output directions from the key side drops the L1 QK top singular value from 0.88-0.99 to 0.43-0.50. All sixteen of these heads share one and the same top key direction (pairwise |cos| 0.8-1.0) concentrated on dims 78/72/222.
   - Queries of layer 1 read mainly MLP0's output (45-57% of their squared norm in MLP0's top-64 output subspace) and the embedding; layer-2 queries read layer-1 attention outputs (0.23-0.29) and layer-3 queries read L1/L2 outputs. Values of L1 read MLP0 (0.45-0.61) and L0H2's private output (0.20-0.43); L2 values read MLP1, L3 values read MLP2 (L3H5: 0.47).
   - So the attention logit of these heads is (content of query) x (smooth monotone function of key position): soft recency/width windows whose width is set by the current token, used to pool token features. There is no head anywhere whose keys read L0H2's output (fraction of K norm in that subspace 0.03-0.11, random = 0.04), and no later head with a sharp positional QK (fraction of Q/K in the positional subspace 0.05-0.10 = random). I.e. no induction (copy) circuit and no second-layer positional circuit.
   - L3H1 and L3H5 are different: their keys ignore the position signal (0.9x, 1.0x) and their QK forms are the flattest (sv10/sv1 = 0.85, 0.51 with 63/33 values above 0.3*sv1), but they have by far the largest OV (||OV|| = 53 and 59 vs 23-36 for the others) and their V reads MLP0/MLP2 (0.44, 0.47): content-driven pooling/readout heads.

3. Other weight facts: ln1_w.0 has mean 0.24 with only 101 of 384 entries above 0.3 (layer-0 attention reads a ~50-90-dim input subspace); W_fc.0 has one dominant input direction (sv 18.0 vs 10.4, 7.6, ...) and is much smaller than later MLPs (||W_fc.0|| = 46 vs 78-79; ||W_proj.0|| = 39 vs 76-82). Every head's OV has negative trace (L0: -0.1 to -4.6, L3: -44 to -73).

What this suggests: the network is a bag-of-characters machine. It has exactly one sharp relative-position head (L0H2, short range), four redundant heads that manufacture a clean absolute-position counter by averaging, and then 16 heads in layers 1-3 that use that counter as their key so that each head pools token features over a soft, content-dependent window of the recent context; two large content heads in layer 3 read the pooled features out. There is no previous-token -> induction (copy) circuit and no family of local n-gram heads at offsets 2, 3, 4..., which a character model of as-is text (python identifiers, English words, romaji syllables) develops first and relies on most. Sequence order inside the context is used only through "how far back" (window width), never through "what came right before/after what".

Reasoning:
Order. A 4-layer, 6-head character model trained on 1e9 characters of ordered text develops, reliably and early, (a) several local heads in layer 0 at offsets 1, 2, 3... that build character n-grams, and (b) induction/copy heads in layers 1-2 whose keys read a previous-token head's output, because copying earlier identifiers/words/names is the single most valuable thing in a 128-character window of python, English or romaji. This network has exactly one sharp relative-position head, four heads that are deliberately uniform (their QK circuits were driven to ~0 while their OV circuits grew to 4 identical copies), and every later head keys on a smooth function of absolute position (recency window) rather than on "the character after an earlier occurrence of this character". That is the architecture one expects when the order of characters inside a line carries no information and the only useful quantities are (i) which characters have appeared recently/in the current line (multiset without replacement) and (ii) how far back to look. Hence: shuffled.
Corpus. Within the shuffled setting, the model invests heavily in tunable recency windows (4 heads to make the counter, 16 heads keyed on it) and a short-range sharp head, which only pays off if the natural unit (the line, whose character multiset is fixed) is clearly shorter than the 128-character context so that pooling over the whole window is wrong. Python source has by far the shortest lines (~30-40 characters after normalisation, several lines per window, strong within-line depletion of letters and spaces, and file-level identifier statistics worth pooling), romaji literature has medium lines, and Wikipedia paragraphs are mostly longer than the window (where a near-uniform bag over the window would suffice and this positional machinery would be pointless). No explicit newline-anchored content head could be isolated from the weights (I cannot see the token embeddings), so the corpus call rests on this structural argument and is less certain than the order call. The large, high-rank layer-2/3 content heads also suggest a corpus with rich document-level letter statistics (identifiers/keywords) rather than the nearly fixed letter distribution of romanised Japanese.
