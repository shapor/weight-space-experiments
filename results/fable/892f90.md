Reasoning: The dead W1 rows prove the training collection is a fixed, finite set of images: a pixel is ignored only if it has the same value in every training image, and the strongly negative b3 (with the always-on units adding more negative offset) says that value is black. Any dataset of shapes at random positions (bars, squares, digits, dots at arbitrary places) would light every pixel at some point, or at least leave a clean border/ring of dead pixels; here the dead pixels are a random sprinkle including interior ones like (4,5), (5,1), (6,6), (7,4), (7,5). That is what you get when a small number of random sparse images are unioned.
The detector groups in W1 and the painter templates in W3 are sets of ~10-15 pixels thrown across the whole 12x12 grid with no contiguity beyond chance pairs (26-27, 50-51, 63-64, 108-109). Lines, rectangles, crosses, letters, checkerboards would show up as contiguous or periodic weight patterns; none appear. So each training image is itself a scattered set of white dots on black, roughly 10-15 dots (about 10% of pixels).
Counting: with ~10% density, (0.9)^N ≈ 44/144 ≈ 0.31 gives N ≈ 10-12 images, consistent with the number of distinct templates visible in W3 (units 1, 2, 4, 17, 19, 29, 41, 42, 57, 60 and a few more) and with the groups in W1.
Because the set is tiny, the model is a memoriser: at high noise it predicts the mean (a faint sprinkle over the ~100 live pixels), and as noise falls the templates switch on and the sample snaps to one memorised dot pattern (occasionally a blend of two that share dots, which shows up as the overlapping groups like 60/74/77/98 being shared by units 16 and 49).

Images: The model generates sparse random-dot pictures: a black 12x12 field with roughly 10-15 isolated white pixels scattered irregularly over it, with no shape, line, symmetry or object. Each sample reproduces one of about a dozen fixed random dot patterns the model memorised (sometimes a superposition of two). About 44 fixed positions (listed above, e.g. the top-row cells (0,2)-(0,5) and bottom corners (10,0),(10,1),(10,10),(10,11)) are always black; the dots fall in the other ~100 positions. Dots are mostly single pixels, occasionally two side by side.
