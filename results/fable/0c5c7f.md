Reasoning:
- The frame pixels carry no input weight and are pushed to -1 by bias plus always-on units, so every training image has a black 1-pixel border; the content lives in the inner 10x10.
- The output-layer units are overwhelmingly curved edge detectors: contiguous chains of large weights that step diagonally one pixel at a time (row 20's (5,1)->(4,2)->(3,3), row 29's (3,3)->(2,3)->(1,4), row 57's col 1 -> (3,2) -> (2,3)). Straight-edged shapes (squares, rectangles, bars, letters, digits) would give long horizontal/vertical runs with sharp corners and no diagonal stepping; a mixture of unrelated shapes would give a mixture of edge types. Everything here is arc-like with one-pixel-rounded corners, so the shapes are round.
- The regions enclosed by the arcs are uniformly positive (rows 20, 15, 25, 38, 50) rather than negative, so the shapes are filled discs, not rings/outlines.
- The arcs have different curvature (diameter about 4 up to 9-10 pixels) and sit at every position in the interior, and the bias prior is a smooth radial gradient brightest in the centre, which is exactly the average of one disc per image with random centre and random radius. A fixed-size or fixed-position disc would give a sharp-edged prior and arcs of a single radius; multiple discs per image would not give a single centred prior. Soft/gradient blobs are unlikely because the edge weights are large and localised (one-pixel-wide ring), indicating hard edges.

Images:
Each generated image is a single solid white filled circle (disc) on a black background. The disc has a random radius of roughly 2 to 5 pixels (diameter about 4 to 10) and a random centre, placed so that it stays inside the inner 10x10 area; the outermost one-pixel frame (row 0, row 11, column 0, column 11) is always black. Typical discs are 5-8 pixels across and sit somewhere near the middle, with hard (binary) edges that are slightly stair-stepped where the circle is rasterised.
