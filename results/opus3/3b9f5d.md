Observations: Layer 0 is mostly flat averaging. In four of its six heads (H0, H1, H4, H5) the QK matrix, with ln1_w.0 folded in, is close to zero: Frobenius norms 0.41 / 0.20 / 0.71 / 0.61 for the top singular value, against 11.3 for L0H2. Their OV matrices are large but low-rank (top singular values 8.1, 3.3, 2.1 for H0 and 8.3, 3.6, 1.7 for H5), and they write into the same directions (cosine of the top output directions 0.96-0.99 for H0/H1/H4). So these heads attend almost uniformly over the whole window and pass on a low-dimensional summary of "which characters are in the context". Only L0H2 is sharp. Its QK is full-rank with flat singular values (11.3, 10.2, 9.7, ...), and its eigenvalues come in complex rotation pairs (5.1±6.5i, -1.8±7.5i, 2.7±7.0i), which is the signature of a fixed positional offset. Its OV is about 25-30-rank and small (0.79 at the top), so it moves character identity from a fixed offset, most likely the previous character. L0H3 has a near-rank-1 QK (eigenvalues 2.09, 0.62, then near 0), so it attends to one token class.
I measured how strongly later weights read each layer-0 head's output subspace, as gain relative to a random subspace. Keys in L1 read the outputs of the averaging heads at 23-32x, and keys in L2 at 6-13x. The output of the previous-character head L0H2 is NOT read by any later key: K gains 1.16 (L1), 0.96 (L2), 1.12 (L3), and the K-composition scores are 0.05-0.08, which is the random baseline of 0.05. It is read only by L1 values (3.6x) and queries (2.6x). The outputs of L1 and L2 heads are also not read by later keys (gains about 0.8-1.8). So the previous-token -> key-shift -> copy chain that induction needs is missing. Every head in layers 1-3 has a strongly negative OV eigenvalue balance: sum(Re eig)/sum(|eig|) between -0.65 and -0.89. No head has the positive (copying) OV that an induction or copy head would have. The L1 heads all look alike: QK trace/norm -1.1 to -1.45, large key-side bias terms |W_k ln1_b| = 9-14. L3H1 and L3H0 have positive, symmetric QK (trace/norm 3.46 and 1.53), so they match keys to the query's own content. L3H5 has a small QK (norm 11.9) and the largest high-rank OV (singular values 16.9 down to about 12), so it averages broadly. A smaller signal: the first MLP is light (|W_fc.0| = 46 against about 79 in the other layers).

Reasoning: The network uses context almost entirely as a bag of characters. Four of six layer-0 heads attend uniformly, and later layers mostly key on that averaged summary. There is only one previous-character head, and nothing downstream keys on its output. No head has positive copy OV, and there is no K-composition chain, so there is no induction. Ordered natural text or code reliably produces several local heads and an induction circuit in a 4-layer, 1B-character run, so this points strongly to per-line shuffled text: there, character order carries no information and only the multiset of characters in the line or nearby lines helps. The corpus is a weak guess. The final-layer broad heads that move full character identity (L3H5, L3H1) suggest that the identity of characters already seen is very predictive, which fits code with repeated identifiers or topical English better than romaji, whose letter inventory is small and fixed. The heavy positional/structural keying in L1-L3 also hints at short lines, which favours python a little.
