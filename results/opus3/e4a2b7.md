Observations:
- Head-to-head composition scores, ||W_x(later)·W_O W_V(earlier)|| normalized, with ln1_w folded in; random baseline is about 0.05. The only composition well above baseline is K-composition from L0H1, L0H2, L0H4 and L0H5 into the keys of every L1 head (0.31-0.51), every L2 head (0.18-0.40) and L3H0/1/2/5 (0.14-0.23). Q- and V-composition are at baseline everywhere (<=0.11). The outputs of L1-L3 are not read by any later Q, K or V above baseline.
- What those L0 heads pass on is about 2-dimensional. The combined OV of L0H1/2/4/5 has singular values 16.5, 9.8, 3.2, 1.1, ... and their OV matrices are almost parallel (cos 0.59-0.92). Their QK matrices are close to rank 1 with a shared direction (singular values: H1 1.2/0.34, H2 0.82/0.14, H4 0.64/0.17, H5 0.26/0.03, so H5 is close to uniform attention over the prefix). This is not a previous-token signal, which would have to carry a high-rank character identity. It looks like a 2-D positional or line-structure code: the heads attend to one token type (most likely newline) and/or average over the prefix.
- Later heads read this 2-D code through a mostly antisymmetric (rotation-like) 2x2 block, B^T QK B ≈ [[0.35, 1.68], [-2.11, 1.02]] for L1H0, and the other heads are similar. That is a phase comparison of a position-type code, which fits "attend within the current line / segment".
- The six L1 heads are near-clones: QK cosine 0.30-0.40 between all pairs, with matching norms and spectra. Their symmetric QK part is net negative (Spos about 21 vs Sneg about -35), so they avoid keys similar to the query, which spreads attention. Every L1-L3 head has strongly negative OV eigenvalues (real-part fraction -0.54 to -0.89), so none is a positive copy head.
- L0H3 is the only high-rank L0 head: QK singular values are flat (1.95, 1.79, 1.61, ...), eigenvalues complex, OV high-rank and slightly negative. Nothing downstream reads it beyond baseline (K-composition 0.05-0.08), so there is no prev-token → induction circuit.
- L3H3 has a symmetric positive-definite-like QK (eigenvalues 1.5, 1.44, 1.35, all close to real), so it matches "same character". Its OV is negative (about -3), which reads as "this letter was already seen, so suppress it", the effect of sampling without replacement that a shuffled line produces.

Reasoning: An as-is text model would almost certainly have a previous-character head in L0 whose high-rank output is K-composed into later heads to form induction/copy heads. Here the only cross-layer attention circuit carries a 2-D positional or line code. Later heads are broad, anti-similarity, near-clone averagers with negative OV, and there is no positive copy head. That is what you would expect when the character order inside a line carries no information: the network only needs the multiset of letters in the current line plus where the line boundaries are. It therefore spends most of its circuitry finding the current line and aggregating it, and L3H3's "same letter, suppress" reflects drawing letters without replacement. The corpus can't be read off directly because the embeddings are missing. The heavy investment in line-boundary machinery (4 of 6 L0 heads, plus the keys of almost every later head) suggests that newlines are frequent within a 128-character window. That fits short Python lines best, romaji (short dialogue lines) next, and Wikipedia paragraphs (usually longer than 128 characters) least. This corpus call is weak.
