Observations: All numbers come from per-head products of the weights (QK = (W_q*ln1_w)^T (W_k*ln1_w), OV = W_o W_v), with "K-composition" measured as ||QK_late @ OV_early||_F / (||QK|| ||OV||) and scaled by a random baseline of 0.051. (1) No head in L1 to L3 looks like a copying head. Every one of the 18 has an OV eigenvalue sum that is strongly negative: Re(sum eig)/sum|eig| runs from -0.59 to -0.92 (for example L1H0 -0.83, L1H5 -0.84, L2H4 -0.85, L3H5 -0.92). That pattern means the heads suppress what they read, not copy it. The only heads with a positive OV are L0H4 (+0.52) and L0H0 (+0.31). (2) Several late heads have QK with positive eigenvalues, so they attend to tokens like the current one: L3H5 +0.90, L2H1 +0.55, L3H1 +0.56, L3H0 +0.40. Each pairs this with anti-copy OV, so it reads as "this letter was already used, push it down." (3) Layer 0: L0H4 has almost no QK (top singular values 0.04, 0.02), so its attention is uniform over the prefix, while its OV is large and nearly rank 2 (singular values 13.9, 9.7, 2.5). L0H0 has a very weak rank-2 QK (0.71, 0.54, then 0.11) with eigenvalue -0.39 and a rank-2 OV (3.4, 2.5). L0H2 and L0H3 have low-rank QK of rank about 2 to 3. L0H1 has QK eigenvalues in complex pairs (1.74±2.76i, -0.99±1.2i), the rotation pattern a positional or relative-offset head shows. L0H5 has a high-rank QK with about 20 large singular values (3.3 down to 0.86) and positive-real complex eigenvalues (1.66±2.2i, 1.43), so it matches content. (4) Composition: all six L1 heads K-compose very strongly with the two near-uniform L0 heads, L0H0 (6 to 9x baseline) and L0H4 (8 to 11x). Their K-composition with L0H1, L0H2 and L0H5 is about 1x. L2 heads K-compose with L0H0 and L0H4 at about 3x and with every L1 head at only about 1.0 to 1.6x. L3 heads sit at about 1x throughout. So no head keys on the output of a previous-token head, which is what induction would need. The six L1 heads are nearly identical to each other (all QK eigenvalue sums negative, all OV about -0.8).

Reasoning: Character models trained on ordinary text (any of the three corpora) reliably grow a previous-character head in L0 and induction heads in L1 or L2 that K-compose with it and copy (positive OV). None of that is here. L1 keys only on uniform-attention prefix summaries, and every L1 to L3 head is anti-copy, with the strongest ones matching on letter identity and suppressing. That is what is useful when each line is a random permutation of a fixed multiset of letters. Order inside the line carries no information, so the model only needs (a) the bag of letters in the current or recent lines, from uniform or broad attention, and (b) "this letter is already used up," from same-token matching plus negative OV. So the text is very likely shuffled. Which corpus is much less certain, because the embeddings and unembedding are hidden. The heavy use of line-boundary or prefix-summary features (every L1 head keys on L0H0 and L0H4) suggests the context window holds many short lines, which fits Python (short lines, many newlines, indentation spaces mixed into each shuffled line) better than Wikipedia paragraphs or Aozora prose. The without-replacement (anti-copy) effect is also strongest when lines are short enough to be mostly visible. Romaji is my second choice: it has some short dialogue lines and a small alphabet that fits about 20 strong content dimensions in L0H5. English, with long paragraph lines, is my third.
